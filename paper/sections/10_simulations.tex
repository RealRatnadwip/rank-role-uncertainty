\section{Synthetic Simulation Study}
\label{sec:simulations}

To examine the quantitative behavior of Relational Superposition Theory across
parameter regimes where analytic closed forms are unavailable or involve finite-size
effects, we conduct four deterministic Monte Carlo experiments using
\texttt{scripts/run\_simulations.py}. Every datum, summary table, and plot in
this section is \emph{synthetic}, generated from the formal mathematical models
of \cref{sec:framework,sec:uncertainty,sec:dynamics,sec:poke,sec:cascade,sec:conjecture}
with fixed PCG64 pseudorandom seeds. No empirical human subject data is used
or implied.

\subsection{Experiment 1: Periodic Attention Drift and Ergodic Convergence}
\label{subsec:sim-markov}

In Experiment~1 (seed \texttt{202601}), we simulate a four-partner portfolio
$\{G_1, G_2, G_3, G_4\}$ over $90$ days ($180$ half-day intervals) using
regime-specific transition matrices $P^{(\mathrm{wd})}$, $P^{(\mathrm{we})}$,
and $P^{(\mathrm{st})}$ (full matrices in \cref{app:reproducibility}), stress
probability $p_{\mathrm{st}} = 0.15$, and EMA smoothing $\alpha = 0.16$.

\subsection{Experiment 2: Superposition Prevalence and Collapse Hazard}
\label{subsec:sim-optionality}

Experiment~2 (seed \texttt{202602}) tests the binomial and Poisson retention
models of \cref{sec:conjecture} across partner counts $n \in \{1, \dots, 10\}$
and attractiveness indices $A \in [0, 10]$, using $4{,}000$ independent trials
per parameter setting. Alongside superposition prevalence, we evaluate a
synthetic $30$-day poke hazard model
\begin{equation}
  h_{30}(n) = 1 - \exp\!\bigl(-0.08\,n\,[1 + 0.12(n - 1)]\bigr),
  \label{eq:sim-hazard}
\end{equation}
capturing both linear individual inquiry risk and quadratic pairwise visibility
friction as partner count expands.

Two qualitative lessons emerge from \cref{tab:cascade-sim}. First, below the

retain partial peripheral insulation ($0.8220$).

\subsection{Experiment 4: Empirical Verification of the Uncertainty Bound}
\label{subsec:sim-uncertainty}

Experiment~4 (seed \texttt{202604}) samples $2{,}400$ random Hermitian rank-role
operator pairs $(\opR, \opL)$ and normalized complex superposed states
$\ket{\Psi}$ across dimensions $d \in \{2, 3, 4, 5\}$ and role-mixing coupling
strengths $\gamma \in \{0.25, 0.75, 1.50\}$. For each state, we evaluate
$\Delta R$, $\Delta L$, their product $\Delta R \cdot \Delta L$, and the
commutator lower bound $\kappa_{\mathrm{comm}} = \frac{1}{2}\abs{\ImPart\braket{\Psi}{\comm{\opR}{\opL}\Psi}}$.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{figures/fig_uncertainty_bound.pdf}
  \caption[Synthetic sweep of the Rank-Role Inequality across random operators]{Synthetic sweep over $2{,}400$ random Hermitian rank-role operator
  pairs and normalized complex states (\texttt{scripts/run\_simulations.py},
  seed \texttt{202604}). (a) Uncertainty product $\Delta R \cdot \Delta L$ vs
  commutator lower bound $\kappa_{\mathrm{comm}}$, with zero points below the
  dashed equality line $\Delta R \cdot \Delta L = \kappa$. (b) Distribution of
  $\Delta R \cdot \Delta L$ across state dimension $d$.}
  \label{fig:uncertainty-bound}
\end{figure}

Across all $2{,}400$ sampled systems, \textbf{zero violations} of~\eqref{eq:rank-role-inequality}
occurred (\cref{fig:uncertainty-bound}a). The observed minimum ratio
$(\Delta R \cdot \Delta L) / \kappa_{\mathrm{comm}}$ over states with
$\kappa_{\mathrm{comm}} > 10^{-9}$ is $1.0000$, confirming that random $2$-dimensional
states routinely approach the minimum-uncertainty boundary of
\cref{prop:minimum-uncertainty}. Across the entire ensemble, the median
uncertainty product is $0.6453$ and the median commutator floor is $0.0860$,
while median dispersion grows systematically with partner count $d$
(\cref{fig:uncertainty-bound}b).

\cref{tab:uncertainty-cells} reveals two geometric trends across the $12$
dimension-coupling cells. First, median rank dispersion $\Delta R$ scales
primarily with portfolio dimension $d$ ($0.42$ at $d=2$ to $1.29$ at $d=5$),

ratios stay close to unity in low dimension ($1.0000$ at $d=2, \gamma=1.50$)
and drift upward in higher dimensions because generic random vectors rarely
lie inside the two-dimensional minimum-uncertainty manifold of
\cref{prop:minimum-uncertainty}.
