01 // THE PROBLEM & DILEMMA

Why classical total orders fail

Multi-role partitioning breaks ordinal ranking

Classical matching theory assumes every participant maps partners onto a strict ordinal list: first choice, second choice, third choice. Under functional partitioning across distinct roles (public visibility r1, late-night emotional support r2, practical provision r3, weekend leisure r4, and reserve contingency r5), no partner dominates vectorwise across the role matrix M.

Changing context weights across days reverses who sits at rank 1. Across 1,000 uniform Dirichlet role weightings on our benchmark 4 x 5 matrix, every partner attains first rank over substantial cones of the simplex (22.7%, 19.0%, 25.3%, 33.0%).

When role evaluation rotates away from the rank basis (L = U(γ)† DL U(γ)), rank and role observables fail to commute, forcing a lower bound via the Schrodinger-Robertson relations. At zero rotation (γ = 0), diagonal operators commute cleanly.

Sub-unit weight alone (wk < 1) does not prevent a unique winner, as shown by w = (0.99, 0.01). Genuine rank-1 indeterminacy requires either tied global maxima (SharedMaximum) or context-driven rank reversal.
