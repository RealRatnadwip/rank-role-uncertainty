\section{Synthetic Computational Experiments}
\label{sec:simulations}

To test internal mathematical consistency across every regime and visualize nonlinear
behaviors not visible from closed-form formulas alone, we run five deterministic synthetic
computational experiments implemented in \texttt{scripts/run\_simulations.py}, paired with
automated mathematical invariant tests in \texttt{tests/test\_rst\_invariants.py}.

\noindent\textbf{Important disclaimer on empirical status.} All numbers, trajectories,
and plots reported in this section are \emph{purely synthetic Monte Carlo or linear-algebraic
outputs} generated with fixed random seeds (\cref{tab:seeds}). None represent real human
survey measurements, and no empirical population parameter is estimated here.

\label{subsec:sim-protocol}

Every experiment accepts explicit deterministic seeds via \texttt{numpy.random.default\_rng}
and emits machine-readable CSV tables plus an audit file \texttt{paper/data/summary.json}.

limitations.

\subsection{Experiment 1: Role-Matrix Normalization and Rank Reversal}
\label{subsec:sim-role-agg}

On the benchmark matrix $M_{4\times 5}$ of \cref{ex:role-aggregation} (Seed \texttt{202605}),
column sums are strictly positive $(14.0, 15.8, 13.5, 15.5, 9.0)$ and each column reaches
a distinct leader ($G_1, G_2, G_3, G_4, G_4$). Under visibility weights
$\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\mathrm{vis}}$, linear scores evaluate to $(0.4167, 0.2225, 0.1716, 0.1893)$
putting $G_1$ first; under support weights $\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\mathrm{sup}}$, linear scores
become $(0.2169, 0.3508, 0.2865, 0.1458)$ putting $G_2$ first. Across $1{,}000$ independent
$\mathrm{Dir}(1,1,1,1,1)$ draws (\cref{fig:role-aggregation}), partner top-rank counts equal
$(227, 190, 253, 330)$, confirming zero universal consensus leader on $\Delta^{4}$.

\subsection{Experiment 2: Periodic Weekly vs.\ Augmented Joint Markov Drift}
\label{subsec:sim-markov}

Using the exact transition matrices $P^{(\mathrm{wd})}, P^{(\mathrm{we})}, P^{(\mathrm{st})}$
in \cref{app:rep-matrices} with $p_{\mathrm{st}} = 0.15$ and EMA smoothing $\alpha = 0.16$
(Seed \texttt{202601}), we compute both the exact $7$-day stroboscopic eigenvector
$\pi_{\mathrm{week}}$, the exact $12$-state joint (partner, regime) marginal stationary
distribution $\pi_{\mathrm{joint}}$, and the empirical visitation frequencies over $180$
simulated days (\cref{tab:markov-stationary}, \cref{fig:markov-drift}). Weekend clustering
pushes stroboscopic $\pi_{\mathrm{week}}(G_4) = 0.3779$, diverging sharply from static support
score $G_4(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\mathrm{sup}}) = 0.1458$, whereas daily stress interruptions
rebalance the $12$-state marginal back toward $G_1$ ($0.3334$) and $G_2$ ($0.3087$).

\subsection{Experiment 3: Optionality Scaling, Heterogeneity, and Attention Budget}
\label{subsec:sim-optionality}

Experiment~3 (Seed \texttt{202602}, $4{,}000$ Monte Carlo replications per grid point) compares
analytic~\eqref{eq:binomial-superposition} against simulated Binomial draws, heterogeneous
Poisson-binomial retention ($p_i$ drawn uniformly around $p=0.45$), capacity-constrained
selection capping active slots at $B_{\max} = 3$, and a synthetic 30-day cumulative
collapse hazard incorporating relational edge growth:
\begin{equation}
  h_{30}(n) = 1 - \exp\!\left(-0.08\,\max(n,1) - 0.015\, n(n - 1)\right).
  \label{eq:sim-hazard}
\end{equation}
All Monte Carlo proportions stay within binomial standard errors of analytic formulas
(\cref{tab:optionality-sim}), while capacity capping flattens expected active partners
sublinearly at $n = 10$ ($3.4947$ kept vs.\ $4.5000$ unconstrained; \cref{fig:optionality-extensions}).

\subsection{Experiment 4: Finite-Graph Cascade Percolation and Transmission Sensitivity}
\label{subsec:sim-cascade}

Experiment~4 (Seed \texttt{202603}, $n = 8$, $\theta = 0.60$, $1{,}500$ trials per edge
density $q \in [0.05, 0.90]$) measures both finite-network cascade probability across
homogeneous ER, clustered two-clique, and hub-mediated graphs at $p_{\mathrm{tx}} = 0.72$,
and transmission sensitivity across $p_{\mathrm{tx}} \in \{0.40, 0.72, 0.90\}$
(\cref{tab:cascade-sim}, \cref{fig:cascade-percolation}). Even at $q = 0.20$ near the
infinite-tree unit reproduction reference $q_{\mathrm{ref}} = 0.1984$ ($R_0 = 1.008$),
finite-$n$ broadening produces $29.33\%$ cascades in homogeneous ER graphs rather than
zero, highlighting why asymptotic thresholds must not be treated as sharp step edges for small $n$.

\subsection{Experiment 5: Unitary Basis-Rotation Uncertainty Sweep}
\label{subsec:sim-uncertainty}

Experiment~5 (Seed \texttt{202604}) evaluates $N = 3{,}200$ normalized Haar-random complex
state vectors across dimensions $d \in \{2, 3, 4, 5\}$ and context rotations
$\gamma \in \{0.0, 0.25, 0.75, 1.50\}$ ($200$ states per cell), setting
$\opR = \diag(1, \dots, d)$ and $\opL(\gamma) = U(\gamma)^\dagger D_L U(\gamma)$ where
$U(\gamma) = \exp(i\gamma H)$ for normalized random Hermitian $H$.

Zero violations occurred out of $3{,}200$ samples for either Robertson ($\min \Delta R \Delta L / \kappa_R = 1.000$)
or Schr\"odinger ($\min \Delta R \Delta L / B_S = 1.000$). Crucially, at $\gamma = 0.0$
where $[\opR, \opL(0)] = 0$, Robertson's commutator bound vanishes identically (\texttt{0.0000}
in every dimension), whereas Schr\"odinger's covariance term tracks the positive dispersion
correlation cleanly (and hits exact equality in $d = 2$ across all $\gamma$). Overall across
nonzero $\gamma$, median observed product is $0.4996$, median Schr\"odinger bound is $0.4409$,
and median Robertson bound is $0.1087$ (\cref{fig:uncertainty-bound}, \cref{tab:uncertainty-cells}).
