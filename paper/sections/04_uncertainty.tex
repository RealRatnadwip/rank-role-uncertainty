\section{The Rank-Role Uncertainty Principle}
\label{sec:uncertainty}

Let $\opR$ denote the rank-measuring operator and $\opL$ the role-measuring
operator applied to a given partner. Informally, measuring rank corresponds to
asking ``where does he stand?'' and measuring role corresponds to asking ``what
does he do?''

\begin{principle}[Rank-Role Inequality]
\label{prin:uncertainty}
For any partner, the dispersion in rank, $\Delta R$, and the dispersion in
role, $\Delta L$, satisfy
\begin{equation}
  \Delta R \cdot \Delta L \;\geq\; \kappaS,
  \label{eq:uncertainty}
\end{equation}
where $\kappaS > 0$ is the \emph{situationship constant}.
\end{principle}

In plain terms: \textbf{you cannot know a guy's rank and his role at the same
time.} If you fix his role precisely (``he is the emotional support''), his
rank becomes undefined. If you fix his rank (``he is first''), his role
distributes across every functional dimension.

\begin{remark}
The value of $\kappaS$ is not universal. It depends on the observed person's
communication style, the size of the social graph, and whether the partners
share mutual friends. We leave its empirical determination to longitudinal
messaging analysis.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Commutation failure.}
The order in which rank and role are queried alters the observed distribution:
\begin{equation}
  \comm{\opR}{\opL} \neq 0.
\end{equation}
