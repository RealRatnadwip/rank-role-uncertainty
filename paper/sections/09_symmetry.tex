\section{Symmetry and the Mirror Configuration}
\label{sec:symmetry}

Although the motivating example introduced a focal person $W$ maintaining
several partners $\{G_i\}$ using the male pronoun for the latter, nothing in
the mathematical machinery of \RST{} encodes gender. This section formalizes
permutation and role-relabeling symmetry and applies the theory to the mirror
configuration.

\subsection{Invariance Under Partner and Role Permutations}
\label{subsec:sym-invariance}

Let $\pi_G \in S_n$ be any permutation of partner indices $\{1, \dots, n\}$ and
$\pi_R \in S_m$ be any permutation of role basis indices $\{1, \dots, m\}$.
These induce a unitary change of basis $U = U_{\pi_G} \otimes U_{\pi_R}$ on
$\vspaceTot = \vspaceG \otimes \vspaceR$.

\begin{proposition}[Unitary and relabeling invariance of \RST{}]
\label{prop:unitary-invariance}
Under any unitary relabeling $U$ mapping $\ket{\Psi} \mapsto U\ket{\Psi}$,
$\opR \mapsto U\opR U^\dagger$, and $\opL \mapsto U\opL U^\dagger$:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item normalization $\braket{\Psi}{\Psi} = 1$ and superposition status
        (\cref{def:superposition}) are preserved;
  \item dispersions $\Delta R$, $\Delta L$, and the situationship constant
        $\kappaS = \frac{1}{2}\abs{\ImPart\braket{\Psi}{\comm{\opR}{\opL}\Psi}}$
        are strictly invariant;
  \item collapse probabilities $\abs{a_i}^2$, renormalization~\eqref{eq:renormalization},
        Markov transition eigenvalues, and social-graph cascade probabilities are
        invariant under index relabeling.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Since $U^\dagger U = U U^\dagger = \opI$, inner products and norms satisfy
$\braket{U\phi}{U\psi} = \braket{\phi}{\psi}$. Thus
$\expect{U\hat{A}U^\dagger}{U\Psi} = \expect{\hat{A}}{\Psi}$ and
$\var{U\hat{A}U^\dagger}{U\Psi} = \var{\hat{A}}{\Psi}$ for $\hat{A} \in \{\opR, \opL\}$.
Similarly, $\comm{U\opR U^\dagger}{U\opL U^\dagger} = U\comm{\opR}{\opL}U^\dagger$,
leaving $\braket{U\Psi}{U\comm{\opR}{\opL}U^\dagger U\Psi} = \braket{\Psi}{\comm{\opR}{\opL}\Psi}$
unchanged. Combinatorial relabeling of vertices in $G_{\mathrm{soc}}$ and rows
in $P^{(r)}$ preserves graph isomorphism classes and spectrum identically.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Mirror Configuration}
\label{subsec:sym-mirror}

In the mirror configuration, a male focal agent $M$ maintains $n$ concurrent
female partners $W_1, \dots, W_n$ (colloquially described as the ``playboy''
configuration). Every primitive object translates without modification:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item role $r_1$ denotes the publicly visible primary partner; $r_2$ denotes
        emotional confidante; $r_3$ denotes practical or domestic support;
        $r_4$ denotes weekend social companionship; $r_5$ denotes low-cadence
        contingency;
  \item prior to a poke, no $W_k$ holds weight $\abs{a_k}^2 = 1$, and
        $\Delta R \cdot \Delta L \ge \kappaS$ holds with identical proof;
  \item a direct status question from any $W_k$ triggers silence, projective
        collapse or exit renormalization, and percolation cascade whenever
        $\{W_i\}$ share mutual acquaintances or social feeds.
\end{itemize}

\begin{proposition}[Gender independence]
\label{prop:symmetry}
Relational superposition is a structural function of optionality $n(A)$ and
social-graph connectivity $q$, not of gender.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Equations~\eqref{eq:partner-state} through~\eqref{eq:conjecture} contain no
gender-indexed parameter, and by \cref{prop:unitary-invariance} any bijection
between focal/partner gender labels leaves all predictions invariant.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Empirical identifiability]
Because the structural laws of \RST{} are symmetric under gender inversion,
observational state data alone (weights, latency, and cascade rates) cannot
distinguish the two configurations without demographic labels. Any population-level
asymmetry observed empirically would therefore reflect differences in the
distribution of $n(A)$, retention probability $p$, or social-graph density $q$
across groups rather than a difference in the governing mechanics.
\end{remark}
